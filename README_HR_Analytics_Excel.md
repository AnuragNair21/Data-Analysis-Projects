# HR Analytics in Excel

This project uses Microsoft Excel to explore workforce composition and selected performance indicators across departments and regions. I summarized 17,417 employee records with pivot tables and dashboard views to make the main patterns easier to review.

## What I explored

- Employee distribution by department, region, gender, education, and recruitment channel
- Average training scores across departments
- The share of employees in each department who met more than 80% of their KPIs

## A few findings

- Sales & Marketing is the largest department in the workbook, with 5,458 employees; Operations follows with 3,524.
- Analytics has the highest average training score at 84.6, closely followed by R&D at 84.5.
- Operations has the largest share of employees meeting more than 80% of KPIs, at 44.1%.
- The records include 12,314 male employees and 5,103 female employees.
- Recruitment channels are recorded as other (9,751), sourcing (7,349), and referred (317).

These are descriptive summaries of the records in this workbook. They show how the measures vary across groups, but do not explain the causes of those differences.

## Workbook contents

The workbook includes a dashboard, employee-level data, and summary sheets covering department, gender, region, recruitment, training scores, and KPI performance.

## Tools and approach

- Microsoft Excel
- Pivot tables and aggregated summaries
- Workforce segmentation and descriptive analysis

## How to explore the workbook

Open the workbook and start with the dashboard for a high-level view. The supporting summary sheets provide the department, regional, demographic, recruitment, training-score, and KPI breakdowns behind the dashboard.

## Data context

The findings reflect the employee records provided in the workbook. Interpret the comparisons in that context; the summaries do not establish why training scores or KPI outcomes differ.
